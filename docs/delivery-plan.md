# Stackjunior Exam Coach Delivery Plan

Version 2.0 · 13 September 2026 · NECO GCE Mathematics

## 1 Planning basis

The plan delivers a reviewed pilot product, runs a six-week paid cohort, measures delayed learning, and makes a documented expansion decision. Requirement identifiers refer to the companion Product Specification version 2.0.

Use two-week sprints. Ten sprints occupy 20 relative weeks from an agreed start. The product reaches pilot readiness at the end of Sprint 6; teaching runs through Sprints 7 to 9; delayed assessment and analysis occur in Sprint 10. This is a sequencing and capacity assumption, not a committed estimate or a promise to meet the original November examination target.

Capacity assumption: two product engineers, a product and design lead, an academic lead, two part-time content reviewers, a shared QA owner, and a named pilot operations owner. People may hold more than one role only after workload is checked. Content production and cohort recruitment run alongside engineering within each sprint. No actual team or partner is assumed to be hired.

Sprint 1 must confirm capacity and re-estimate the plan. If the available examination runway is shorter than the programme plus delayed measurement, recruit for a later verified session or explicitly redesign the pilot. A separately scoped manual learning experiment can precede the software; it does not mean the software release is ready.

## 2 Milestone overview

| Milestone | Sprints and relative weeks | Goal | Required exit decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 Scope and evidence foundation | S1 to S2, weeks 1 to 4 | Confirm the cohort, teaching scope, reviewed content and demand hypothesis | Approve a specific, feasible pilot design or revise scope before deeper build |
| M2 Initial learner route | S3 to S4, weeks 5 to 8 | Deliver onboarding, diagnostics, evidence state and an explainable plan | Demonstrate reliable routing across representative learner cases |
| M3 Complete pilot workflow | S5 to S6, weeks 9 to 12 | Add teaching, early assessments, review, reporting and operational readiness | Approve enrolment only after learning, content, privacy and service gates pass |
| M4 Six week teaching pilot | S7 to S9, weeks 13 to 18 | Deliver the programme and collect protected outcome evidence | Complete teaching and endline without changing the experiment retrospectively |
| M5 Evidence and investment decision | S10, weeks 19 to 20 | Measure delayed performance and review demand, quality and economics | Proceed, iterate or stop, with evidence and limits recorded |
| M6 Conditional expansion | After M5, separately estimated | Extend coverage and test richer teaching without weakening the evidence rules | Each extension passes its own release gate |

A milestone passes on evidence, not elapsed time. An unmet critical criterion blocks that release, while independent work may continue. Product owns scope and commercial decisions; the academic lead owns content and measurement; engineering and QA own behaviour and service evidence; operations owns recruitment and support readiness.

## 3 Milestone acceptance gates

### M1 Scope and evidence foundation

Goal: establish what is being sold, to whom, what is taught, and how success will be measured.

- M1-AC1: A versioned NECO GCE Mathematics configuration records the verified session, syllabus, paper references, marking requirements and source-check dates.
- M1-AC2: The complete syllabus is mapped, 12 to 18 pilot skills are selected, unsupported areas are labelled, and reviewed content meets the Sprint 2 inventory gate.
- M1-AC3: Demand discovery and actual reservation/payment evidence support one proposed channel, buyer, package and price; weak demand is recorded rather than treated as validation.
- M1-AC4: Product, academic and operations owners accept the pilot protocol, recruitment calendar, staffing allocation and cost ceiling. Unresolved assumptions have an owner and due date.

### M2 Initial learner route

Goal: produce a useful plan without overstating what a short assessment establishes.

- M2-AC1: A new learner can complete disclosed onboarding, pause/resume the diagnostic and receive a plan within the selected time budget.
- M2-AC2: Strong, weak-prerequisite, sparse-evidence, conflicting-evidence and interrupted-session cases produce the expected evidence state and explainable route.
- M2-AC3: No screen converts practice accuracy into a predicted examination score or describes sparse evidence as proven mastery.
- M2-AC4: Academic reviewers can inspect and reproduce the evidence and policy behind each recommendation.

### M3 Complete pilot workflow

Goal: prove the complete student loop and establish safe, supportable pilot operations.

- M3-AC1: A student completes probe, teaching, independent check, scheduled review, early mixed assessment and targeted repair. Failure to improve also produces a usable next step.
- M3-AC2: A content defect can be disputed, quarantined, corrected and propagated to learner records with an audit trail.
- M3-AC3: Protected assessment content cannot enter ordinary practice, tutor retrieval or generated variants; access and allocation tests pass.
- M3-AC4: Consent, supporter access, payment disclosures, withdrawal handling, recovery and support procedures pass rehearsal. No unresolved critical marking, data access or answer-loss defect remains.
- M3-AC5: At least 30 paid eligible learners are ready for the frozen cohort start. If fewer enrol, formally revise the protocol and expectations before any baseline rather than silently relaxing this gate.

### M4 Six week teaching pilot

Goal: deliver six weeks consistently and preserve interpretable evidence.

- M4-AC1: All six teaching weeks and the endline assessment are delivered, with attendance, assistance, additional tuition, incidents and support effort recorded.
- M4-AC2: Weekly decisions preserve the frozen outcome definitions, assessment allocations and comparison conditions. Any essential change is versioned and its analysis effect recorded.
- M4-AC3: Engagement and quality targets are reported using their defined denominators; dropouts and nonresponders remain visible.
- M4-AC4: Critical content or access failures are contained immediately. Affected data and learners are identified, with resumed operation justified by verification.

### M5 Evidence and investment decision

Goal: decide whether the product deserves expansion on the basis of independent learning, demand and operating cost.

- M5-AC1: The delayed assessment, data-quality review and planned analysis are complete, including missing-data and subgroup limitations.
- M5-AC2: The report compares fresh unaided outcomes, describes uncertainty, and does not substitute an internal mastery metric for the primary outcome.
- M5-AC3: Actual revenue, support and content labour, delivery cost and acquisition cost are reconciled; repeat-cohort projections expose every assumption.
- M5-AC4: Product and academic owners record proceed, iterate or stop. Public claims match the evidence; score forecasts and guarantees remain disabled unless their separate gates are met later.

## 4 Sprint 1 Scope demand and protocol

**Goal:** Choose a buildable NECO GCE Mathematics pilot and define the commercial and learning hypotheses before implementation expands.

**Owners:** Product lead, academic lead and operations. Engineering supports feasibility and data design.

**Dependencies:** Access to current official examination information, candidate interviews and a prospective recruitment channel.

**Work and deliverables:** Exam configuration; syllabus outline; pilot skill shortlist; interview synthesis; a draft fixed-duration offer; initial content inventory; protocol; staffing and cost assumptions; risk and decision register.

- S1-AC1: The academic lead records the exact target session and applicable source references with verification dates. A conflict between dates and the 20-week plan results in a revised cohort/start decision.
- S1-AC2: Interview at least 10 prospective learners and 5 prospective payers, recording current study behaviour, alternatives, device access, time constraints and objections without treating stated interest as payment evidence.
- S1-AC3: Test one concrete package and price with at least five actual paid or refundable reservations under clearly disclosed terms. If unavailable, mark demand unresolved and constrain further investment explicitly.
- S1-AC4: Select 12 to 18 pilot skills with their prerequisites, rationale, exclusions and teaching/assessment inventory. No broad full-subject claim appears in the offer.
- S1-AC5: A draft protocol defines six teaching weeks, delayed assessment, primary outcome, comparison approach, participation denominators, consent, missing-data handling and proposed targets.
- S1-AC6: Every role has a named owner or an explicit staffing gap. The cost model records labour rates, content effort, delivery assumptions and an approved spending ceiling before paid operation.

**Review evidence:** Scope decision, interview and reservation summary, exam sources, candidate skill map, protocol and re-estimated schedule.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-01, REQ-18, REQ-19, REQ-20.

## 5 Sprint 2 Content and assessment foundation

**Goal:** Build the reviewed instructional and measurement foundation that all later personalisation depends on.

**Owners:** Academic lead and content reviewers; engineering owns storage and publication controls.

**Dependencies:** Sprint 1 exam configuration, skill scope and permission to use content.

**Work and deliverables:** Versioned curriculum map, question import and review workflow, prerequisite graph, item-family model, practice bank, protected forms, diagnosis exemplars and content quality checklist.

- S2-AC1: Every syllabus area is mapped to supported, planned or excluded-for-pilot status; the interface wording and programme offer use the same scope.
- S2-AC2: Each pilot skill has at least 12 published practice items across at least three question families plus a reviewed explanation and worked example. Items have all REQ-12 metadata and an accountable reviewer.
- S2-AC3: Two comparable 30-item baseline/endline forms, six 15-item weekly forms and one 20-item delayed form are reviewed and protected. Academic review documents coverage, difficulty balance and the limits of each form.
- S2-AC4: An exposure audit finds no exact items or close variants shared between protected outcome forms and practice/teaching content. Protected forms have explicit permitted users, sessions and release rules.
- S2-AC5: Publication rejects missing answers, rationale, family, provenance or reviewer records. Quarantining a question stops new delivery but retains the published version used in historical attempts.
- S2-AC6: Prerequisite validation rejects cycles and academic reviewers approve the links. Confirmation probes include alternative valid methods and ambiguous-error examples.

**Review evidence:** Content inventory, form blueprints, provenance register, exposure audit and publication/quarantine demonstration. If reviewers cannot complete the bank, change scope or timing rather than populate gaps with unreviewed generated material.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-09, REQ-12, REQ-13, REQ-14, REQ-22.

## 6 Sprint 3 Onboarding and initial diagnosis

**Goal:** Let a learner begin with informed expectations and obtain an honest initial assessment.

**Owners:** Engineering, design and QA; academic lead reviews diagnosis behaviour.

**Dependencies:** Approved content and assessment rules from Sprint 2; consent and reporting decisions from Sprint 1.

**Work and deliverables:** Accounts, age-appropriate consent path, optional supporter relationship, examination and availability setup, protected diagnostic delivery, pause/resume, attempt recording and initial evidence summary.

- S3-AC1: Before enrolment, the learner or payer can read coverage, commitment, price, assessment allocation and absence of a guarantee. Adult candidates can complete onboarding without a guardian account.
- S3-AC2: A configured diagnostic runs within its academic time/item cap, offers bounded probes and saves every response. Closing and reopening the session restores the last saved answer and marks interrupted timing.
- S3-AC3: A correct guess scenario remains uncertain; one incorrect option does not confirm a misconception; unassessed skills stay unassessed.
- S3-AC4: Attempt records include item version, family, assistance, exposure, timing context and assessment allocation. Retried submissions create one accepted attempt and one corresponding event.
- S3-AC5: A learner cannot read another learner's answers; an unauthorised supporter cannot read reports; revoked supporter access is denied on the next access check.
- S3-AC6: A diagnostic summary states covered skills, observed results and missing evidence without a whole-exam score prediction.

**Review evidence:** End-to-end onboarding and recovery demonstration; access-control checks; reviewed cases for sparse evidence, guessing and interruption.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-01, REQ-02, REQ-04, REQ-08, REQ-17, REQ-22, REQ-23, REQ-24.

## 7 Sprint 4 Evidence states and daily planning

**Goal:** Turn observed evidence into an achievable, inspectable daily route.

**Owners:** Engineering and academic lead; product and QA approve learner-facing explanations.

**Dependencies:** Reliable attempts and scope-aware diagnostic summary from Sprint 3.

**Work and deliverables:** Evidence eligibility rules, versioned state updates, rule-based activity selection, time budgeting, due reviews, learner overrides and recommendation history.

- S4-AC1: State updates enforce independent-attempt, family, session and delayed-review requirements from REQ-08. Assisted or near-duplicate success cannot independently produce retained or transfer evidence.
- S4-AC2: Plans for 15, 30 and 45 minutes fit their configured budget. An activity that cannot fit is shortened into a valid unit or replaced, not silently allowed to overrun the planned budget.
- S4-AC3: Confirmed blocking prerequisites can redirect the selected activity; the learner can still open another supported skill and assessment mode.
- S4-AC4: Every selected activity exposes a plain-language reason and stores the candidate set, evidence references, duration assumption and policy version.
- S4-AC5: Changed availability, repeated skips and missed reviews produce a feasible recovery plan. The old commitment remains in history, and target changes require explicit learner action.
- S4-AC6: Academic-reviewed cases cover strong learner, weak prerequisite, slow-but-accurate learner, conflicting observations, sparse data, no due reviews, exhausted item pool and unsupported skill. Expected decisions are specified before running the checks.

**Review evidence:** Reproducible learner scenarios, before/after plan history and interface review for misleading certainty. No marks-per-hour forecast or opaque seven-factor multiplier is required.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-03, REQ-07, REQ-08, REQ-09, REQ-10, REQ-11, REQ-22.

## 8 Sprint 5 Teaching independent checks and early assessment

**Goal:** Deliver the complete gap-investigation and teaching loop, then check performance without assistance.

**Owners:** Engineering and academic lead; reviewers audit mathematical behaviour.

**Dependencies:** Sprint 4 evidence and planner; Sprint 2 reviewed explanations and protected item pools.

**Work and deliverables:** Bounded tutor context, hints and explanations, guided/independent modes, misconception hypotheses, early mixed checkpoints, timed sections, review scheduling and mock-to-skill repair.

- S5-AC1: For an ambiguous error the tutor asks a probe or clarification; a confirmed prerequisite gap receives the relevant intervention. Alternative valid methods are accepted in the reviewed scenario set.
- S5-AC2: Completing a guided example does not count as an independent check. Revealing a hint updates the attempt context before evidence is recomputed.
- S5-AC3: After two unsuccessful explanation routes, the learner receives a prerequisite, break or human-review option. No infinite teaching loop or compulsory progression through every difficulty exists.
- S5-AC4: A new learner can open a mixed or timed pilot-scope assessment before finishing the route. The assessment displays its scope, and errors generate repair suggestions without resetting unrelated skills.
- S5-AC5: Fresh checks use eligible item families; the delayed review is scheduled and cannot be marked retained before the delay and evidence count are satisfied.
- S5-AC6: Protected assessment items are denied in tutor and practice retrieval tests, including a request for an unreleased form. Generated teaching context cannot draw from the protected pool.
- S5-AC7: Tutor failure serves a reviewed fallback or review request while retaining student work. A stratified academic review of at least 100 simulated tutor interactions reports correctness, ambiguous diagnosis and answer-revealing behaviour; critical defects are corrected before pilot use.

**Review evidence:** Complete learning loop demonstration; early assessment access; protected-content checks; academic review report and failure/recovery demonstration.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-04, REQ-05, REQ-06, REQ-09, REQ-13, REQ-15, REQ-17, REQ-24.

## 9 Sprint 6 Reports review and pilot readiness

**Goal:** Make the product supportable and approve a concrete, fully disclosed paid cohort.

**Owners:** Operations, product and QA; engineering owns corrections, reporting and service verification.

**Dependencies:** Complete Sprint 5 learning loop; recruitment interest developed from Sprint 1 onward.

**Work and deliverables:** Weekly reports, reminders and recovery, dispute console, question quarantine and corrections, payment/withdrawal records, usage-cost tracking, support playbook, release checklist and frozen pilot protocol.

- S6-AC1: A report separates participation, supported practice and independent results; it shows the agreed versus revised commitment and offers one practical recovery action. Supporter sharing respects current permission.
- S6-AC2: A disputed answer can be corrected; affected evidence and recommendations recompute; the original attempt and correction remain traceable; the learner receives the correction.
- S6-AC3: A full rehearsal covers enrolment, consent, payment disclosure, allocation, baseline, practice, checkpoint, lost connection, tutor outage, support and withdrawal. Retention/deletion and incident procedures have owners.
- S6-AC4: Test keyboard access and mathematical readability on the declared phone and desktop profiles. Answer recovery and duplicate-submit prevention pass. Service latency is measured against REQ-24 under the recorded connection profile and twice expected peak concurrency.
- S6-AC5: Actual usage and staff time can be attributed to the programme and cohort without exposing sensitive details in tutor logs. The cost model includes content, support and acquisition labour.
- S6-AC6: At least 30 paying learners are eligible and ready; refund/withdrawal handling and comparison allocation are explained before payment. Product records a start/no-start decision against the frozen criteria.
- S6-AC7: A five-learner usability rehearsal using separate rehearsal items produces no unresolved task-blocking problem. Research outcome forms remain unexposed. There is no unresolved critical marking, access-control or answer-loss issue.

**Review evidence:** Signed release record, readiness checklist, cohort roster counts, frozen protocol, cost report, support rota and complete rehearsal results. Pilot recruitment and payments are future team tasks; this document does not execute them.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-07, REQ-12, REQ-16, REQ-18, REQ-19, REQ-20, REQ-22, REQ-23, REQ-24.

## 10 Sprint 7 Pilot weeks one and two

**Goal:** Start the cohort successfully, collect clean baseline evidence and establish repeat practice.

**Owners:** Operations and academic lead; engineering remains available for reliability issues.

**Dependencies:** M3 release approval and the cohort start decision. Freeze assessment and analysis versions before the first baseline.

**Work and deliverables:** Consent confirmation, protected baseline, disclosed group allocation, first two teaching weeks, two weekly checkpoints, activation report, incident review and actual usage-cost report.

- S7-AC1: All starters have recorded consent, disclosed price and allocation, and access to their assigned service. The common unaided baseline precedes adaptive diagnostic probes and intervention exposure; later probes cannot change its score. Deviations are flagged and retained for analysis.
- S7-AC2: Activation is calculated as defined in the specification; target at least 75 percent within seven days. Missing activation triggers documented outreach and barrier investigation without coercion or hidden exclusion.
- S7-AC3: Both weeks' sessions and checkpoints are delivered according to the protocol. Assistance and protected-item exposure are recorded; the comparison group receives its promised service.
- S7-AC4: Support requests and mathematical defects have owners, severity, response times and resolution/update status. Critical issues suspend the affected feature or item immediately.
- S7-AC5: Usage, staff time and cost are reported against the Sprint 1 budget. Operational fixes are versioned; the primary outcome and success thresholds are unchanged.

**Review evidence:** Activation and exposure report, incident log, first cost reconciliation and protocol deviation register.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-16, REQ-18, REQ-20, REQ-21, REQ-24.

## 11 Sprint 8 Pilot weeks three and four

**Goal:** Test sustained participation and investigate whether the programme fits real student routines.

**Owners:** Operations and product; academic lead reviews retention and content quality.

**Dependencies:** Two delivered teaching weeks and a complete exposure log.

**Work and deliverables:** Weeks three and four, checkpoints, within-programme delayed reviews, weekly supporter reports, interviews with active and inactive learners, mid-pilot quality/cost review.

- S8-AC1: Week four participation uses all paid starters as the denominator; target at least 60 percent completing two substantive sessions. Withdrawals and technical access failures are separately visible.
- S8-AC2: Median adherence is reported against each learner's documented agreed plan, including revisions; target at least 60 percent. Plans are not retroactively reduced to improve the reported percentage.
- S8-AC3: Delayed review failures update the plan without deleting earlier observations. The interface distinguishes an overdue review from a failed review.
- S8-AC4: Interview at least five active and five inactive/withdrawn learners where available; record nonresponse if the inactive group cannot be reached. Separate learning, affordability, device and scheduling barriers.
- S8-AC5: Review a stratified sample of live teaching and marking interactions, prioritising disputed and ambiguous cases while also sampling routine successes. Publish rates with the sampling method and keep the cohort protocol unchanged except documented essential fixes.

**Review evidence:** Participation/adherence report, interview synthesis, quality audit, cost trend and list of interventions made within the frozen protocol.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-07, REQ-08, REQ-16, REQ-18, REQ-20, REQ-21.

## 12 Sprint 9 Pilot weeks five and six

**Goal:** Complete the programme and measure fresh, independent endline performance.

**Owners:** Academic lead and operations; engineering supports assessment reliability.

**Dependencies:** Four teaching weeks and validated outcome form allocations.

**Work and deliverables:** Final teaching weeks and checkpoints, protected endline, programme-completion report, dropout reasons, external tuition record and delayed-assessment appointments.

- S9-AC1: All six weekly programmes and checkpoints are accounted for. Noncompletion, support used and additional tuition are retained in the analysis dataset.
- S9-AC2: Endline is unaided and uses the learner's assigned fresh form under comparable conditions. A disconnect, hint exposure or suspected form leakage is flagged under predeclared handling rules.
- S9-AC3: Do not publish success claims from completers alone or show endline improvement as an official NECO score gain.
- S9-AC4: Schedule the delayed assessment approximately seven days after endline. Record intervening study and hold comparison-group adaptive access until that assessment is complete.
- S9-AC5: Reconcile paid, started, withdrawn, baseline-complete and endline-complete counts; unresolved discrepancies block final analysis.

**Review evidence:** Locked endline export, form exposure audit, participant flow counts and delayed-assessment schedule.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-13, REQ-17, REQ-20, REQ-21, REQ-22.

## 13 Sprint 10 Delayed evidence and investment decision

**Goal:** Determine what was retained, what the programme cost and whether expansion is justified.

**Owners:** Product and academic lead; operations reconciles costs and payments; an analyst or suitably qualified evaluator reviews the analysis.

**Dependencies:** Complete endline data and the approximately seven-day delay. If the assessment window slips, move the decision rather than shorten the delay silently.

**Work and deliverables:** Protected delayed assessment, de-identified analysis dataset, outcome report, cost model, claim review, decision record and the next bounded backlog.

- S10-AC1: Baseline, endline and delayed completion are reported for all paid starters; target at least 80 percent with the full measurement set. Missing data, reasons and sensitivity analyses are included.
- S10-AC2: Analyse the primary outcome specified before the pilot, with baseline adjustment and uncertainty intervals; separate randomised from observational conclusions and describe additional tuition and differential dropout.
- S10-AC3: Evaluate the proposed directional signal of at least 5 percentage points at endline and no negative delayed difference against the comparison. Wide intervals or missing evidence produce an inconclusive judgement, not a claim of proven effectiveness.
- S10-AC4: Reconcile net revenue, refunds, AI/hosting/payment usage, recurring content/support labour and acquisition cost. Present actual pilot contribution separately from repeat-cohort projections and fixed development costs.
- S10-AC5: Review at least 100 live interactions in total across the pilot using the declared stratified method; report confirmed material defects and unresolved issues. Critical academic or access defects prevent expansion until resolved.
- S10-AC6: Record proceed, iterate or stop with the evidence for learning, demand, participation, quality and economics. Deliver the comparison group's promised subsequent access after protected measurement is complete.
- S10-AC7: The next backlog is bounded by the findings. No forecast, guarantee, whole-subject claim or new examination is enabled automatically.

**Review evidence:** Final pilot report, reconciled costs, uncertainty and missing-data appendix, claims checklist and expansion decision.

**Requirement coverage:** REQ-17, REQ-18, REQ-19, REQ-20, REQ-21.

## 14 Conditional expansion sprints

These are separately gated planning blocks, not an extension automatically committed at week 21. Estimate them after M5 using observed engineering, content and review throughput. Each engineering sprint retains the two-week cadence; content volume or outcome collection may require several such sprints or an entire exam cycle.

### Expansion A Complete Mathematics coverage

**Goal:** Progress from a focused pilot to a supportable subject product.

**Suggested sequence:** One sprint to scope and audit the remaining skills; repeat content-and-teaching delivery sprints for bounded skill groups; one integration sprint for verified full-paper simulations and full-subject reporting. Determine the number of content sprints from the actual syllabus gap and reviewer throughput.

**Acceptance criteria:** Every advertised skill passes REQ-12 to REQ-16; full-paper forms match the verified examination configuration; fresh forms remain protected; the coverage dashboard accurately distinguishes taught, supported and unassessed areas; any learner can access a ready mock without completing all topic gates; workload and support costs remain within the revised package model.

### Expansion B Voice and live whiteboard

**Goal:** Test whether richer explanation improves comprehension or usability enough to justify its cost.

**Suggested sequence:** One sprint for an internal prototype with reviewed lessons; one sprint for interruption, pause/resume, text fallback and instrumentation; a bounded learner trial spanning enough sessions to compare unaided understanding with the text experience.

**Acceptance criteria:** Speech and board preserve the exact question and mathematical steps; interruption stops both and resumes at a coherent point; uncertain learner speech prompts clarification; failed audio falls back to text without lost work; the academic benchmark has no unresolved critical error; measured latency, accessibility and cost meet thresholds agreed before trial; independent learning is compared with the simpler mode rather than judging only enjoyment.

### Expansion C Handwritten and spoken working

**Goal:** Capture useful reasoning evidence without silently misreading the learner.

**Suggested sequence:** One sprint for a representative benchmark and confirmation UX; one or more implementation sprints based on benchmark results; one release-validation sprint with target users.

**Acceptance criteria:** Preserve the original work; show uncertain transcription for student confirmation; test alternative valid methods, accents, poor images and incomplete steps; unresolved transcription cannot change independent evidence; correction propagates through marking and planning; accuracy, false diagnosis, consent, retention and processing cost meet thresholds frozen before evaluation.

### Expansion D English and other examinations

**Goal:** Add a new domain with its own assessment validity.

**Suggested sequence:** One discovery sprint per exam/subject; content and assessment sprints sized after that audit; a separate measured pilot before broad launch.

**Acceptance criteria:** Verify the examination configuration and content sources; define a subject-specific skill taxonomy, scoring rubrics and protected assessments; validate passage or extended-response marking where required; review accessibility and content costs; do not reuse Mathematics mastery thresholds or score mappings without evaluation.

### Expansion E Score forecasting

**Goal:** Produce useful, calibrated forecasts only after verified outcome data exist.

**Suggested sequence:** A data-quality sprint; a protocol and simple-baseline sprint; model/evaluation sprints once sample size and held-out data are adequate; a monitoring and limited-release sprint. Collecting actual exam outcomes is an external calendar dependency, not a two-week engineering deliverable.

**Acceptance criteria:** An evaluator agrees data sufficiency and the exact forecast unit before model evaluation; learner and cohort separation prevent leakage; the model beats or justifies itself against a simple baseline on the frozen held-out evaluation; intervals have assessed coverage; subgroup errors and missing official results are reported; material uncertainty suppresses the forecast; marketing explains the validated population and limitations.

### Expansion F Outcome guarantee

**Goal:** Evaluate whether a fair and financially supportable guarantee should exist at all.

**Suggested sequence:** One policy and eligibility design sprint after forecast validation; a financial and operational review block; a closed-cohort experiment over the relevant official examination/result period; a decision sprint after claims are resolved.

**Acceptance criteria:** Define the result unit, locked commitment, workload limits, refund scope, outages, verification and appeal route; validate forecasts for the eligible cohort; independent review approves clear terms and bounded exposure; the platform cannot increase requirements retrospectively; claims and refunds can be audited; scale only after actual claims experience supports the policy. A decision not to offer a guarantee is an acceptable outcome.

## 15 Shared definition of ready and done

**Ready for a sprint:** A story identifies its learner or operator, observable goal, requirement IDs, owner, content dependencies, failure cases and acceptance evidence. Academic rules and comparison conditions are specified before implementation. If an assumption is unresolved, the story is an experiment with a decision output rather than a falsely certain feature.

**Done for a sprint:** The agreed acceptance criteria pass in the intended environment; relevant mathematical and access checks are complete; misleading certainty and scope claims are absent; analytics and recovery work; documentation reflects the released behaviour; no unresolved critical defect affects the feature; the owner records evidence and remaining noncritical limitations.

Critical defects include incorrect marking that materially misleads learners, unauthorised disclosure, lost accepted answers, protected-form leakage and an inability to complete the core learning/assessment workflow. Ordinary content corrections still need provenance and affected-record review even when they do not suspend the entire pilot.

Test behaviour with meaningful cases and independent expected results. Avoid tests that merely restate the implementation. Every pilot release must exercise support removal, delayed review, ambiguous diagnosis, valid alternative methods, changed availability, early assessment access, connection failure, correction propagation and permission revocation.

## 17 Decisions to close before commitment

The first examination is settled as NECO GCE Mathematics. Sprint 1 must close the exact session, initial learner band, recruitment partner, price, content access, staffing and programme start. Sprint 2 closes the assessment blueprint. Sprint 6 freezes the final protocol, support schedule and cohort roster before baseline exposure. None of these decisions should be silently filled in by an AI forecast or a development estimate.

The practical first investment is M1. Its output should be concrete enough to decide whether the proposed cohort, content workload, learning measurement and commercial offer justify the remaining build.
