# The approved reading text, paper by paper

UTME Use of English sets five questions on a novel that JAMB announces for each cycle and then
replaces. The syllabus itself does not name it — the note on `ENG.A.4` says so — but each paper
does, in a line printed above the questions. Those lines are the only record we have, and they
are transcribed here as they appear.

| Paper | Questions | Text named in the paper |
|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 21–30 | Chukwuemeka Ike, *The Potter's Wheel* |
| 2013 | 31–35 | Jerry Agada, *The Successors* |
| 2014 | 21–30 | Chukwuemeka Ike, *The Potter's Wheel* |
| 2014 | 31–35 | Jerry Agada, *The Successors* |
| 2015 | 21–36 | A. H. Mohammed, *The Last Days at Forcados High School* |
| 2016 | 21–35 | A. H. Mohammed, *The First Days at Forcados High School* |
| 2017 | 16–25 | S. I. Manyika, *Independence* |
| 2011, 2012, 2018 | one each | no attribution line survived the extraction |

The 2015 and 2016 titles are taken from the questions themselves, which name Forcados High
School throughout; the attribution lines for those two years are cut short in the source at
"based on A", and the author's initial is all that is left of them.

## Why the questions built on these are retired

A question on *The Potter's Wheel* cannot help a candidate sitting a paper set on a different
novel, and the text changes every cycle. That is unlike every other kind of question in this
bank: a synonym question from 2011 is as good in 2027 as it was then, and a reading-text
question is worth nothing the moment the text is replaced. Licensing the novels would not fix
it either — the problem is the cycle, not the copyright.

So the 69 questions set on these texts are retired rather than held. What is worth keeping is
this table and the questions themselves, which show the shape the examiner uses: recall of
incident, of character, and of a line's speaker. When a current text is announced and
licensed, they are the model to write against.
